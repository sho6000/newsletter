Sunbird RC is wallet ready

Sunbird RC is how you stand up a registry and start issuing digital credentials without building the hard parts yourself. You describe what you are recording. RC handles the schemas, the signing, the storage and the verification. What you get is a record you can trust, and credentials the people in that registry can carry and prove anywhere.

RC is a recognised Digital Public Good, the engine inside DIVOC for vaccination credentialing, and part of the registry layer behind DIKSHA. Open source, MIT licensed, free to adopt.

Wallet ready: RC v2.1.0
Sunbird RC just shipped v2.1.0. The short version: credentials from your deployment now work with wallets you did not have to build.

Built on the standards the ecosystem settled on
RC now speaks OpenID for Verifiable Credentials for issuance and presentation, and supports W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 alongside 1.1. Your credentials point the same direction the rest of the world is moving. All of it is opt in: no database migration, no change to what you already run.

Any wallet, not just one you built
Until now, getting an RC credential into a wallet meant building against Sunbird's own APIs. Someone had to own that integration, and it only ever worked for the one wallet it was written for. Now any conformant wallet can receive a credential and present it back to an RC verifier, with nothing Sunbird specific at the wallet end. It has been tested against wallets built outside the Sunbird ecosystem.

Holders share only what is needed
Credentials can be issued in formats that support selective disclosure, including the mobile document format behind digital driving licences. Someone can prove they are over 18, or that they hold a qualification, without handing over the rest of the credential. Less exposure for the holder, less data for you to protect.

What you can build with it
Anywhere a credential is issued once and has to be trusted every time it is shown afterwards, there is a registry underneath. Vaccination and health records, training and skilling certificates, learner credentials that follow a student between institutions, professional licences, farmer and land records, organ pledge registries, proof of participation in a programme. The shape is always the same: a verified record becomes something the holder carries, instead of a document they have to request again each time someone asks for it.

A number of these already exist as reference solutions and working demos, so you can follow a credential from issuance through to verification before committing to build anything of your own.
